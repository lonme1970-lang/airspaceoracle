Map the coordination problem
Illustrative workflow—not an authorization checklist
  1. Define the proposed activity, operating area, and time window.
  2. Separate assumptions from confirmed information.
  3. Identify affected users and relevant authorities.
  4. Compare options against stated constraints.
  5. Record unresolved decisions and the next coordination action.
Actual requirements depend on the location, activity, and authorities.
Competing requirements
Fictional educational scenario
Two teams request activity in the same demonstration area and time window. No real location, altitude, or operating approval is provided.
Information gaps
Boundaries, activity characteristics, decision-makers, communication arrangements, and contingency requirements.
Options to examine
  • Separate activities in time.
  • Revise operating areas, subject to assessment.
  • Reduce demonstration scope.
  • Defer until essential information is available.
The initial requests are not an executable plan. Resolve gaps and coordinate with responsible authorities. Not advice for a real flight.
From constraints to a decision brief
Illustrative 45-minute workshop—not qualification or certification
  1. 5 minutes: introduce the fictional scenario and objectives.
  2. 10 minutes: identify assumptions, constraints, and gaps.
  3. 15 minutes: develop two options and tradeoffs.
  4. 10 minutes: present a recommendation and coordination needs.
  5. 5 minutes: discuss what remains unresolved.
Learning check
Can participants distinguish an assumption from a requirement, identify decision responsibility, and explain recommendation limits?

Execution & Ground Truth
Airspace Is More Than a Control Measure
Core Question: What should planners understand about connecting airspace plans to real-time execution?
A control measure drawn on a digital map is an administrative intention, not an operational reality. In complex environments, the challenge is rarely drawing the polygon—it is managing the dynamic boundaries when weather degrades, communications degrade, or emergent fires demand priority. Planners must design airspace architectures with built-in deconfliction thresholds rather than relying on constant, fragile manual coordination.
Transferable operational perspective. Does not disclose classified, controlled, or proprietary event details.
Capability Integration
Demonstration Is Not Integration
Core Question: What questions should an organization ask before treating a demonstrated capability as operationally integrated?
A successful demonstration in an optimal test window often obscures operational reality. Before declaring a system field-ready, leaders must ask: What does this demand of the tactical airspace stack? Who resolves the airspace conflicts when the automated link fails? How does the operator maintain situational awareness without screen saturation? True integration begins where the demonstration ends.
Transferable operational perspective. Does not disclose classified, controlled, or proprietary event details.
Civil-Military Integration
Shared Airspace Starts with Shared Understanding
Core Question: How do planners establish coordination across organizations with fundamentally different responsibilities?
Civil air traffic management prioritizes predictable, commercial throughput and passenger safety. Military operations require agility, rapid reservation, and tactical realism. Bridging these two domains cannot rely on ad-hoc phone calls during an exercise. It demands bilateral coordination frameworks, agreed buffer matrices, and joint liaison procedures that protect civil flow while enabling high-tempo training.
Transferable operational perspective. Does not disclose classified, controlled, or proprietary event details.
Operational Sustainment
Before We Add Another System, How Will We Power It?
Core Question: What power and sustainment questions belong in capability planning from the beginning?
Every new digital airspace tool, tactical radar, and counter-UAS defeat mechanism brings a hidden logistic tail: kilowatt draw, generator maintenance, thermal signature, and fuel consumption. Capability planning must integrate power budgets and field survivability on day one, ensuring advanced tools do not collapse the tactical support footprint.
Transferable operational perspective. Does not disclose classified, controlled, or proprietary event details.
Workforce Development
From ATC to Airspace: Bridging the Planning Gap
Core Question: What additional knowledge prepares an ATC professional for brigade, division, and field-army responsibilities?
Air traffic controllers are masters of separation, speed control, and rapid tactical decision-making inside a designated facility. However, operational airspace management at division and field-army levels requires a different mindset: integrating fires, uncrewed swarms, sensor coverage, and host-nation corridors into a unified theater plan. Bridging this gap requires systematic operational education beyond FAA/DoD facility ratings.
Transferable operational perspective. Does not disclose classified, controlled, or proprietary event details.
Doctrinal Architecture
Why I Developed TALAS: The Problem Before the Framework
Core Question: What operational problem motivated the concept, and what can be understood without exposing sensitive details?
Traditional airspace control measures were designed for manned aircraft flying between known waypoints at thousands of feet. The proliferation of low-altitude s-UAS, loitering munitions, and commercial urban air mobility collapsed that paradigm. TALAS originated from a simple operational realization: low-altitude airspace requires dynamic, echeloned volume allocation rather than static, bloated airspace coordination areas.
